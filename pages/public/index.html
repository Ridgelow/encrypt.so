01 — What it is

Private by design

encrypt.so is a mobile messenger for two people. You write on your phone. The app seals the message, then sends the sealed copy.

  1. 01

    Sealed before it leaves

    Encryption runs on the device. The readable message stays between the two phones in the conversation.

  2. 02

    One conversation, two people

    This version is one-to-one. Each chat has its own session, built when the first message is sent.

  3. 03

    A timer, when you want one

    Set a disappearing-message timer and the note is removed on both phones when it runs out.

02 — End-to-end

How a message stays sealed

Your phone locks the message. Their phone unlocks it. The server holds the locked copy and does not have the key.

  1. 01

    Keys live on the phone

    Setup creates a key pair on the device. The private key stays in the phone’s secure storage. encrypt.so keeps the public key, which is what someone needs to start a conversation with you. You can compare a safety number in person to confirm that key belongs to the person you mean.

  2. 02

    The first message agrees on a secret

    The two phones run a key agreement called X3DH. They arrive at a shared secret without sending that secret across the network. That exchange opens the session.

  3. 03

    Every message gets its own key

    The session moves forward with each message. That step is the Double Ratchet. A key copied later cannot open messages that were already sent. That property is forward secrecy.

  4. 04

    The server moves a sealed envelope

    encrypt.so stores ciphertext — the scrambled message — and the small amount of metadata needed to deliver it to the right device. The words are not in the database.

03 — Privacy

Plaintext stays on the phones

  • The server never sees plaintext

    The message is encrypted on your phone before upload. What lands in storage is a sealed envelope.

  • Private keys stay on the device

    They sit in the phone’s secure storage. They are not in the API and not in a backup we hold.

  • We cannot open a chat

    There is no master key. A request for the words cannot be answered from our systems, because the words are only on the phones in the conversation.

  • Metadata stays small

    Next to the ciphertext is enough information to route a message to a device. The contents of the message are not part of that record.
